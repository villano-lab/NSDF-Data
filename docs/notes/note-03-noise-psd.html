Restricting the 21 traces to Channel 0, the best-represented channel, leaves 14: 12 from Detector 0 and 2 from Detector 1 (events 10708 and 10959). The channel filter did not separate the detectors, so this PSD mixes two detectors. Note 7 shows why that matters.

Why the two selections do not overlap

Applying the original cut to Detector 0 / Channel 0 alone gives 12 traces, none among the 41. All 12 are quiet baselines: with the first 10 samples skipped, as in Note 2, their bstd is a median of 1.56 (3.84 as originally computed) and their ratio is 3.3–6.1 (1.6–2.4 as originally computed). That places them in the broad hump of log10(ratio) 0.5–1.0 of Note 2 (here 0.52–0.79). Note 2’s first cut instead requires ratio < 1.93, which selects the contaminated near-zero spike, so the two cuts pick out different populations.

The original cut also has a selection bias. Its low ratio comes from a bstd inflated by the leading samples. Among the 868 quiet Detector 0 / Channel 0 traces (bstd < 2.5 with the glitch skipped), 160 have an original bstd more than 1.3× the glitch-skipped value and 708 do not. The original cut kept 12 of the 160 and none of the 708: it selects quiet traces whose leading samples inflate bstd, and systematically excludes equally quiet traces without that. (All of this is computed in the last section of good_pulses_overlay.ipynb.)

Time domain

Each trace is baseline-subtracted with the mean of its own pretrigger window. A couple of the traces have a single-sample startup transient in the first few dozen samples, a known quirk of this digitizer data rather than a real pulse. The plot’s y-axis is clipped to the 0.5–99.5 percentile of all plotted samples so the transient does not wash out the noise texture.

Frequency domain

The real FFT (np.fft.rfft) is taken of every trace. The sample period is 1.6 µs (625 kHz sample rate, supplied by the user; it is not recorded elsewhere in the repository), so with 4096 samples per trace the bins are 152.6 Hz wide and run up to the 312.5 kHz Nyquist frequency. The power spectrum is spectrum × conj(spectrum) (its imaginary part is zero by construction), plotted log–log with the DC bin dropped. Averaging the 14 spectra bin by bin gives a first noise PSD estimate:

The average falls steeply from about 8×106 at 150 Hz to ~2×105 near 2–3 kHz, stays roughly flat out to ~20 kHz, then rolls off to a floor near 104 above ~100 kHz. Several sharp peaks (near 26, 42, 78, 105 and 204 kHz, among others) sit on top of it. Where those peaks come from is the subject of Note 7.

Conventions and caveats

  • The plotted quantity is the raw NumPy |FFT|2, not a normalized one-sided PSD in units per Hz. Golwala’s SLAC thesis (Appendix B, “Noise and Optimal Filtering”) puts a 1/N on the forward transform, opposite to NumPy, so the absolute scale here is not directly comparable to it.
  • The leading glitch samples are kept in the traces that go into the FFT. Baseline subtraction skips them only when computing the mean it subtracts; it does not remove them. This was a deliberate choice.
  • The selection is small (14 traces), mixes two detectors, and is biased toward quiet traces with glitch-inflated bstd (see above). The sharp peaks in the average come from its two Detector 1 traces (Note 7).
