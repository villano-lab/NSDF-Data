Goal and setup

Pick out clean noise traces on Detector 0, Channel 0 of series 07221203_2025, dump F0001 (Run76: Side2, PuBe, Pb&poly lead stack and borax, −83.2 V), as the input to a noise-PSD study. The dump has 4551 channel groups; Detector 0 / Channel 0 contributes 1517 traces of 4096 samples each.

Per trace, from a pretrigger window at the start of the trace: bmean and bstd are the mean and standard deviation of the pretrigger samples, dev is the largest |sample − bmean| in the samples after the pretrigger window, and ratio = dev / bstd. Some traces have a brief (1–5 sample) electronic step glitch at the very start, so the first 10 samples are skipped when computing bmean and bstd. That fixes the glitch but not a real pulse that reaches into the pretrigger window.

dev is measured only after the pretrigger window on purpose. Measuring it over the same samples that set bstd puts a hard floor near ratio ~ 1 whatever threshold is used, which made an initially requested threshold of 0.5 impossible to reach.

First cut, and why it is misleading

The first “good noise” cut kept traces with bstd below its own 75th percentile and ratio below its own 25th percentile (bstd < 32.48, ratio < 1.93): 41 of 1517 traces. Plotting log10(ratio) for all traces shows two features: a sharp spike near 0 and a broad hump from about 0.5 to 1.4.

Repeating the calculation with the pretrigger window shortened from 1000 to 500 samples changes the picture:

  • the near-zero spike disappears;
  • the broad hump stays where it was, so it does not depend on the window length;
  • a new bump appears near log10(ratio) 2.0–2.4: pulses that start between sample 500 and 1000 are correctly seen as excursions with the shorter window, but were swallowed into the baseline with the longer one.

The reason is extreme-value statistics. dev is the maximum over roughly 3000 samples, so even pure Gaussian noise lands around ratio ~ 3–8 (log10 0.5–0.9), not near 1. A trace only reaches ratio ~ 1 if its own bstd is inflated, i.e. its pretrigger window already contains part of a pulse or a slow drift. The low-ratio spike is contamination, not quiet noise — the naive “smaller ratio means quieter” expectation is backwards.

Branch 1: the 1000-sample window

Widening the near-zero slice to log10(ratio) in [−0.05, 0.48) gives “Cut A” (417 traces); the broad hump [0.5, 1.0) on the same window gives “Cut B” (892 traces).

Unverified. The rise-time result in this subsection rests on pulse_quantities.rise_sample, a half-maximum-crossing heuristic that is tagged under_development and has only been used on this one analysis. Treat it as a working hypothesis. In the notebook this section is collapsed by default and carries the same warning.

Hypothesis: if the Cut A pulses have similar amplitude, then the later a pulse rises, the less of the pretrigger window it contaminates, so bstd is smaller and ratio larger. Estimating a rise sample per trace (first post-pretrigger sample crossing half of dev) over all 417 traces gives a Pearson correlation of 0.45 between ratio and rise sample, with rise samples spanning 1000–2698. The scatter has two components: a floor at exactly 1000 (pulses that already crossed half-maximum inside the pretrigger window, so the method reports the window boundary), and a genuine rising trend above it (ratio 1.3–2.9 with rise sample 1200–2700). Consistent with the hypothesis, but not independently verified.

Status / Next Steps

  • The tightened cut is now a library function (excursion_band, with excursion_band_loose for [0.5, 1.0)); see Note 4.
  • The 13 real pulses in the tightened selection are removed by a pulse-shape veto in Note 6.
  • The rise-time hypothesis (Branch 1) is still unverified; rise_sample needs checking against noisy and multi-peak traces first.
  • Everything here is one detector and one channel of one dump. Other channels and other dumps have not been done (Channel 2 was 100% dead in this dump, so it needs a separate look).
  • The cut boundaries (0.5, 0.7, 1.0, 1.4) were chosen by looking at plots, not derived from a model of the noise.
